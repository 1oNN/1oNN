01 · CONCEPT

The game is played on a lamp-lit oak craft table

Every piece of interface is a thing on that table: a linen blocking mat under the work (with a faint gauge grid), a printed pattern leaflet, paper tags for the stitch keys, a sewing pin marking the last release on the tension meter, a beech hook with a 5 mm size mark, and a wound ball of yarn that shrinks as you use it. Everything structural is a warm neutral (oak, linen, cream paper, walnut ink); the four yarn colours and one clay accent are the only saturated things on the table, and the accent only ever points at the next action. Stitches are plied yarn, not chart symbols, so a sloppy row looks sloppy; the chart symbols live in the keys and chips so the game quietly teaches chart reading. After dark the same table sits under a lamp: darker wood, warmer key light, and a small halo on the yarn so it stays the brightest thing in the room without turning into neon.

  • Things, not widgetsMat, leaflet, tags, pin, hook and ball. If it is on screen, it could be on the table.
  • Neutral room, colourful yarnOak, linen, paper and walnut ink carry the structure; only yarn is saturated.
  • One accent, one next actionClay marks the step to take now, and nothing else competes with it.
  • A lamp, not neonAfter dark the halo is rationed to yarn strands and meter fills, never the light theme.

02 · PALETTE

Day table and lamp-lit table, one set of tokens

Each chip shows the day value on the left and the lamp value on the right, read from docs/design-tokens.css (inlined in this page). CSS, the 2D canvas and the 3D scene all read these same custom properties.

The swatches are built from the token block by a script.

Contrast, computed from the hex values

WCAG 2.x relative luminance · text needs 4.5:1, titles on wood 7:1, rings and strokes 3:1

Two pairs are kept off text on purpose in daylight: --muted and --accent on the wood (links on wood are ink with an accent underline). Yarn is never text; on the light mat its contrast comes from the warm shadow pass every strand is drawn with, which is what you see in the glyph sheet below.

04 · STITCH GLYPHS

Five stitches, four ways to make them

Drawn live on these canvases by drawStitch() from js/render.js, the function the game, the title-screen guide and the social card all use, with tension t −0.9 for loose, 0 for neat and +0.9 for tight. The column heads are the chart symbols the keys and chips carry (STITCH_SYMBOLS).

Day · linen mat
Lamp · linen mat
  • NeatUpright, even width, a whole cap loop on top. A ±0.6 px wobble keeps a perfect row hand-made.
  • LooseWider and taller, leaning, paler at 82%; the cap sags 2 px and a slack loop hangs off the base.
  • TightNarrower and shorter, leaning the other way, 22% darker, a pinched cap. Reads “pulled hard”.
  • WrongBrick red in stripes with a sewing pin pushed in. Never colour alone: shape, stripes and pin all say it.

After dark the halo pass switches on (--halo-canvas: .3), and runs at 1.3× on a wrong stitch only.

06 · THE 3D VIEW

The same work, lying on the table

Another view of the same game, not another game: press V and only the mat changes. Each stitch is a yarn tube built from the 2D layout, so both views always agree on where every stitch is and where the hook stands. three.js r160 is vendored and loaded only when a player picks 3D.

  • Hook in the last loopExactly where the 2D chart puts it, so a wrong stitch is on the hook and frogging pulls it back a stitch.
  • Follows the rowThe camera pans to each new row and the ball travels beside it, so its shrinking still warns you.
  • Cheap where it must beDraws only when something changes; phones get a stepped-down pixel ratio and lighter materials.

07 · SINCE v0.1

What changed from the first prototype

v0.1 (the first commit) played the same game on a flat cream page. The rules, patterns and scoring are unchanged; everything you see is new.

Look and world

  • A flat cream page with white cardsan oak craft table: paper leaflets, a linen blocking mat with a gauge grid, paper tags, washi tape and a rubber stamp.
  • Pink on the title, turn, frog and nextone clay accent that marks only the next action; status colours mark only quality.
  • Nunito named in CSS but never shippedthree system stacks (serif display, UI body, mono), nothing fetched.
  • A lamp-lit night theme on N, remembered per device, with every contrast pair checked in both themes.

Fabric

  • 4 px line symbolsplied yarn in shadow, body and highlight passes; tension changes width, height and lean, so a sloppy row looks sloppy.
  • hdc drawn with a bar, dc with two (a treble)chart convention fixed: hdc is a plain post, dc has one bar, sc is two legs under a cap.
  • A wrong stitch was a dashed red outlinered, striped and pinned, and it stays on the hook until frogged.
  • Hook at the left edge, yarn arcing over the worka beech hook in the top loop of the last stitch; the working yarn runs down to a ball that shrinks as yarn is used.

Controls and feedback

  • Plain text buttonspaper tags with chart symbols and keycaps; the expected tag is tinted with a NEXT flag and chevron, and a blocked game dims the tags and flags frog NOW.
  • A thin tension baran 18 px trough with loose / neat / tight ticks, a knob while holding, a pin at the release and a readout in words and seconds.
  • Leaflet chips carry chart symbols and quality colours, a highlighter band marks the current row, and an “Uh oh” card explains a wrong stitch.
  • Completion gets a rubber-stamp grade, a results list and personal bests, plus an out-of-yarn ending.

Reach

  • Phones: every play control above the fold at 390 × 844, five tags in one row, and phones held sideways.
  • A 3D view on V (vendored three.js r160, loaded on demand) and a 3D showcase page.
  • The smoke test now covers 360 / 375 / 390 px, sideways phones and the 3D switch; the shot scripts regenerate every picture on this board and fail on visual regressions they can measure.
08 · OPEN QUESTIONS

Decisions we would like help with

None of these blocks the prototype. The first three are carried over from the brief; the rest came from looking at the captures on this board.

  1. Does the single crochet read as a stitch, or as a triangle?

    The sc is now two legs to an apex under a cap (night-yarn’s path) instead of craft-room’s plump arch.

    Proposal: ask crocheters in playtest; if it reads as a triangle, bow the legs 0.10 w instead of 0.06 w before going back to the arch.

    Decide in playtest

  2. Should the ball stay at the lower right?

    Odd rows end at the right edge, so their turning chains finish near the ball.

    Proposal: if they crowd it, move the ball half a cell further right, never back over the work.

    Decide in playtest

  3. Is the lamp halo strong enough on cheap screens?

    The halo runs at 35% / .3, in the dark theme only.

    Proposal: raise it to 45% / .4 only if yarn looks dull on low-end LCDs; never add it to the day theme.

    Decide on devices

  4. Which view should a new player see first?

    2D is the default and the choice of V is remembered. 3D is the more striking picture, the chart the more legible one at phone size.

    Proposal: keep 2D first; invite players to try 3D from the completion card after their first finished piece.

    Stakeholder call

  5. The leaflet’s row list cuts a line in half

    On the desktop play screens the list stops part-way through a row (“Row 8” is half visible above the stats).

    Proposal: a soft fade at the bottom edge, and keep the current row second from the top as rows are done.

    Design

  6. 3D on a phone: the table, or the piece?

    In the showcase at 390 × 844 the whole mat fits, so the work is small and most of the screen is table.

    Proposal: a portrait camera that brings the work to about 80% of the width while playing; keep the wide table for the completion turntable.

    Design + engineering

  7. Skein bands under the lamp

    The paper bands on the project-card skeins use --surface, which is dark brown after dark, so in 2d-cards-dark.png they read as dark stripes rather than paper labels.

    Proposal: give the bands a fixed cream in both themes, as real skein labels would be.

    Design (small)

  8. Does strike-through help or hurt the leaflet?

    Done rows are struck through, row number included, so the top half of the leaflet is mostly crossed-out text by the end of a project.

    Proposal: strike the instruction only and keep the row number plain, or swap the strike for a small tick.

    Design
